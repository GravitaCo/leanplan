# Seedance prompts: ready-made plan exercises (slice A)

Prompts for the 28 exercises used by Tali's three ready-made plans. They follow
`docs/exercise-video-prompts.md` (the house doc).

## How to build each prompt

1. Paste the **character and scene block** from the house doc exactly as written.
2. Paste the prompt below. Where an exercise uses a machine or cable, the prompt already
   includes the house doc's machine line with the machine name filled in.
3. Each prompt already ends with the right ending line and timing text:
   - **Rep clips** end with the house doc's rep ending line: "Two slow, controlled repetitions,
     perfect form, no bouncing or swinging. She starts and ends in the same position." That is
     followed by the timing text, built on the house tempo (about 3 s lowering, a short pause,
     about 1.5 s lift, a brief hold, so about 13 s for two reps).
   - **Hold clips** use this ending instead: she gets into position, holds for about 8 to 10
     seconds and returns, so she starts and ends in the same pose.
   - **Steady clips** use the house doc's steady-pace ending word for word, but the length
     is set to about 8 to 10 seconds.

## Where this differs from the house doc (please sense-check)

- **Hold length.** The house doc's plank prompt starts already in the plank and holds for about
  20 s. This brief asks for getting into position, a hold of about 8 to 10 s, and returning, so
  the hold prompts below do that. They run about 13 to 16 s in total.
- **Steady length.** The house doc says 10 to 12 s for steady clips. This brief says 8 to 10 s,
  and the prompts below use 8 to 10 s.
- **March on the spot** is logged as `hold`, but it is a timed movement with no position to
  hold. It is written as a steady clip. If it should really be a hold clip, say so.
- **Farmer carry** is logged as `weight-reps`, but a carry has no reps. The two-rep ending does not
  fit, so this prompt shows one carry: pick up, a short walk out and back, put down. It uses an
  adapted ending line. It is the hardest clip here for a video model to get right. Please check
  how the app counts a carry (steps, distance or time) before generating it.
- **Shoulder rolls** does not fit the house lower-and-lift tempo. It uses a slow roll timing
  that I chose, and it is marked as a judgement call.
- **Machine and cable moves** (leg press, leg curl, lat pulldown, seated cable row, face pull)
  are not home-friendly. They are in the plans, so they get the house doc's machine line. Face
  pull also allows a band, which would need a clip of its own.
- **Reused house wording:** Leg press, Dumbbell shoulder press, Incline dumbbell press, Lat
  pulldown, Seated cable row, Face pull and Dead bug use the house doc's prompts and timing text,
  with small additions where the app's cue asks for more. Dumbbell bench press adapts the house
  doc's chest press (dumbbell version).

### Bird-dog (`bird-dog`)
- Clip file: `bird-dog.mp4` · Type: rep
- Equipment in scene: thin grey mat
- Prompt (paste after the character and scene block):
  > Side-on low shot at floor level. She kneels on all fours on a thin grey mat, hands under her
  > shoulders and knees under her hips, back flat and neck neutral, looking at the floor. She
  > slowly reaches her right arm forward and her left leg back until both are level with her
  > body, keeping her back flat and her hips level, pauses, then returns to all fours. She repeats
  > with her left arm and right leg. Her leg never lifts higher than her body, so her lower back
  > does not arch.
  > Two slow, controlled repetitions, perfect form, no bouncing or swinging. She starts and ends
  > in the same position.
  > 2 slow repetitions, one each side, in about 13 seconds. Each rep is a 3-second reach of the
  > opposite arm and leg, a 1-second pause at full reach, a 1.5-second return, and a 1-second
  > pause on all fours.
- Form check: lower back arching or leg lifted above hip height; hips tipping or rotating to one
  side; same arm and leg reaching instead of opposite ones.
- Notes: alternates sides within the set like the house dead bug, so both sides are shown rather
  than mirrored.

- Equipment in scene: thin grey mat
- Prompt (paste after the character and scene block):
  > Side-on low shot at floor level. She lies on her back on a thin grey mat, knees bent, feet
  > flat and hip-width apart close to her bottom, arms resting on the mat by her sides. She

  > shoulders, squeezes her glutes at the top, then lowers her hips slowly back to the mat. Her
  > weight stays on her upper back, not her neck, and her lower back does not over-arch at the

  > mat.
- Form check: hips pushed past the shoulder-knee line so the lower back arches; weight rolling
  onto the neck or head; heels lifting.

### Incline push-up (`incline-push-up`)
- Clip file: `incline-push-up.mp4` · Type: rep
- Equipment in scene: one sturdy flat bench
- Prompt (paste after the character and scene block):
  > Side-on wide shot. The whole body is in frame from head to feet, because the legs and hips matter in this move. The equipment she uses is a sturdy flat bench. Her hands rest
  > on the edge of the bench a little wider than her shoulders, arms straight, feet together on
  > the floor behind her, legs straight and knees off the floor, body in a straight line from head to heels. It is clear she is not kneeling. She lowers her chest
  > towards the edge of the bench with her elbows angled back, not flared out, then pushes
  > herself away until her arms are straight again. Her hips stay in line with her shoulders
  > throughout, neither sagging nor piked up.
  > Two slow, controlled repetitions, perfect form, no bouncing or swinging. She starts and ends
  > in the same position.
  > Starting with her arms straight, 2 slow repetitions in about 13 seconds. Each rep is a
  > 3-second lowering to the bench edge, a 1-second pause, a 1.5-second push away, and a 1-second
  > pause with arms straight.
- Form check: her feet or knees out of frame; hips sagging or piking up; elbows flaring straight out to the sides; the bench
  sliding or looking unstable.
- Notes: the cue also allows a worktop or step, and holding dumbbell handles for straighter
  wrists. The clip shows the plain bench version with hands flat, to keep it simple.
