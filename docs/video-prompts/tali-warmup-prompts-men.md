## Step 2: warm-up prompts

### 1. March on the spot with arm swings (`march-on-the-spot`)

- Clip file: `warmup-march-on-the-spot.mp4` · Type: warm-up
- Equipment in scene: none

```text
Front three-quarter medium-wide shot. He stands tall and marches on the spot at an easy pace, lifting his knees a comfortable height and swinging his arms loosely and naturally, shoulders relaxed. His breathing picks up a little but he stays relaxed and comfortable, not pushing. He moves at a steady, easy pace the whole time, relaxed and breathing comfortably, as if he could hold a conversation. The clip loops, so his pace and position look the same at the start and end. About 8 to 10 seconds, roughly one step every half second to second, starting and ending with his feet together and arms by his sides.
```

- Form check: marching fast, jogging or bouncing; stiff arms or shrugged shoulders; drifting across the frame so the loop jumps.
- Notes: a pulse raiser, so it uses the steady ending rather than a rep count. "Starting and ending with his feet together" is added so the loop closes; a mid-stride match would also work. If Seedance reads "hold a conversation" as talking, drop that phrase.

### 2. Step jacks (`step-jacks`)

- Clip file: `warmup-step-jacks.mp4` · Type: warm-up
- Equipment in scene: none

```text
Front medium-wide shot. He stands tall with his feet together and arms by his sides. He steps his left foot out to the side as his arms rise out to the sides to a comfortable height, about shoulder height or a little above, then steps back in as his arms come down. He then does the same with his right foot. His steps are light, both feet stay close to the floor and he never jumps. A steady, even rhythm. Two easy, controlled repetitions each side, perfect form, no bouncing or swinging. He starts and ends in the same position. Starting standing tall, left, right, left, right in about 8 seconds, a steady rhythm of about 2 seconds per step: about 1 second stepping out as his arms rise and about 1 second stepping back in as they lower.
```

- Form check: he hops or jumps; his arms are flung up or forced overhead; the rhythm is uneven or his feet stamp.
- Notes: the brief says one each side for alternating moves, but one each side at a warm-up rhythm is only about 4 seconds, so this shows two each side to reach the clip length. Cut to one each side if a shorter loop is fine. The cue says "only as high as is comfortable" without a height; "about shoulder height or a little above" is my judgement, chosen so the clip doesn't model a full overhead reach.

### 3. Arm circles (`arm-circles`)

- Clip file: `warmup-arm-circles.mp4` · Type: warm-up
- Equipment in scene: none

```text
Front medium shot, framed from the hips up. He stands tall, feet hip-width apart, arms straight out to the sides at shoulder height. He draws small circles forwards with his arms, the second a little bigger than the first, then draws two circles backwards, small then a little bigger. His shoulders stay low and relaxed, away from his ears, and the circles stay within a comfortable range. Smooth and continuous, no flinging. Two easy circles each way, perfect form, no jerking or forcing. He starts and ends in the same position. Starting with his arms out to the sides, 2 circles forwards and 2 backwards in about 9 seconds, roughly 2 seconds per circle with a brief settle as he changes direction.
```

- Form check: his shoulders hunch up towards his ears; the circles become big, fast windmills; his arms drop well below shoulder height.
- Notes: the cue says "let them grow" but doesn't say whether the backward circles also grow; I have them start small again. With two circles each way the growth will be subtle. Where his arms go at the start and end (out to the sides) follows the cue's set-up.

The house ending's "no bouncing or swinging" contradicts this move, so it says "no jerking or forcing", as in the library prompt.
