# Guide "Keeping it steady after a big change": learning rules

Binding for the design agent. `mental-performance`, 18 Oct 2026. Builds on docs/plans/keep-it-steady.md.

## 1. Duolingo principles

Keep:
- **Bite-size lessons**, one idea each.
- **Active recall.** One short question after reading beats rereading for later recall (Roediger and Karpicke 2006; Adesope et al. 2017). Evidence is from education, not health apps.
- **Immediate, explained feedback**: say why, not just right or wrong (Shute 2008).
- **Spaced review**, with no storage (see 2).
- **Progress as position only**: "Lesson 3 of 8".

Drop: streaks, day counts, records, "keep pushing"; XP, points, badges, confetti (trivial, short-lived lifts that turn understanding into a score); hearts or lives; leaderboards or any comparison; "finish your lesson" reminders.

**Instead of a streak:** a lesson map of eight tiles. A read tile gets a soft tick for this visit only; unread tiles look plain, never empty or "missed". At the end: "You've read all eight. Come back to any of them whenever it helps."

## 2. Understanding checks

- **Format:** tap one of 2 or 3 options, usually a short scenario ("Which helps most on a hungry day?"). No typing, voice or timers.
- **Content only.** Never ask about the person ("Are you still taking it?"). Never test fear: no question whose answer is "you will regain".
- **Right:** "That's it." plus one line of why.
- **Wrong:** neutral "Not quite.", then the right answer and why, then Continue. No red, cross, shake or sound; no retry required. Highlight the right option in tint, never the chosen one in red.
- **Skippable:** a quiet "Skip" on every check.
- **How many:** one per lesson, two at most. No end test.
- **Stored or scored: nothing.** No score shown; no answers or completion saved locally or in Supabase; nothing in analytics, push or AI prompts. Progress lives in memory and resets on close; lessons stand alone, so nothing resumes.
- **Spaced review without storage:** the Maintain weekly review (keyed on goal and plan week, already stored) may show one "Worth remembering" card from the general lessons (sleep, hungry days, strength). Never a medication line, never a quiz.
- **Gentle mode** (wellbeing Yes): checks become "Which feels most useful to you?" with no wrong answer. No numbers in any question, option or reply.

## 3. Lesson structure

Eight lessons, 3 to 5 cards plus one check, under 90 seconds each. "Need support now?" on every screen.

1. **Your appetite may feel louder.** Normal, says nothing about you, no timeline. First card: "Any change to a medicine is for you and your prescriber."
2. **Steady is success.** Most people regain some weight; Tali helps with habits, not a promised result. Weight as a range over weeks.
3. **Strength keeps what matters.** Short sessions help you keep muscle.
4. **Protein at each meal.** 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg, shown as plates first.
5. **Sleep and hunger.** Short nights can make hunger louder. Pick a wind-down time.
6. **A plan for hungry days.** Ends by offering an if-then plan (user's choice; wording never names a medicine).
7. **A drift is the signal working.** Options, not fixes. Includes: "If eating feels out of control or food thoughts are distressing, talk to your GP. Beat can help too."
8. **A new starting point.** A valid choice, not giving up.

Gentle version: lessons 1, 3, 5, 6 and a support lesson. No 2, 4 or 7; no numbers.

## 4. The inspirations

1. **Speech-to-text sheet.** Take the calm entry sheet (icon, title, two explainer rows, ink pill) as the guide's opening, with rows like "8 short reads" and "Nothing you read or answer is saved". Take large, airy type, one idea per card, but in Geist 28 to 34: a serif is off-system (flag to Benn). Avoid voice input and the waveform.
2. **Stats home.** Take one plain headline per lesson, topic chips, cards in the pillar fills (mind, food, move). Avoid scores, percentages, "above average", "doing well 78%".
3. **Week streak.** Take only the calm dots, as lesson-map position. Avoid the rest.
4. **Calm finance.** Take the centred one-liner that names the hard part, then the steady view ("Hungry days happen. A plan helps more than holding out."), and the calm dark mode. Avoid hero numbers and "Slide to invest" (a new pattern that pressures commitment; use the ink pill).
