## 1. The answer in one minute

**Top 3 alternatives (screening level only, none is cleared):**

1. **Damson.** The cleanest screen of the 64 names I looked at: no live US marks in the health or software classes, no health app found, and it is the plum colour Tali's identity already uses, so the logo, palette and tone carry over.
2. **Lagom.** The best meaning: Swedish for "just the right amount", which is "ranges, not rules" in one word. But it is a known Swedish word (weak in the EU), there is a US LAGOM mark in medical-services class 44, and IKEA ran "Live Lagom" in the UK.
3. **Mosey.** Warm, unhurried, the opposite of gym culture, easy to say. But a Cardiff walking-audio app called Mosey launched in August 2026 and mosey.com is a US compliance-software company.

Reserve: **Tarn** (a still mountain lake: calm and very British), held back because a small "Tarn: private health journal" app sits in the App Store's Health & Fitness category.

**Keep Tali?** [I] Viable only for a UK-first launch, and only if a UK attorney confirms there is no UK or EU "TALI" registration covering software or health services. The two Talis Benn named are not the biggest problem. The bigger one is a third: **TALI Health Pty Ltd** (Australia, a CE-marked attention-training app for children). It holds live US registrations for the word TALI in classes 9, 10, 41, 42 and 44 (registered 2020). Also, Tali AI's iPhone app is listed in the same Health & Fitness store category Tali would use. A store descriptor helps users tell the apps apart. It does little for the trade mark risk.

**Confidence: low to medium.** I could check US trade marks, domains and app search properly. I could not search the UK IPO or EUIPO registers directly (both blocked automated access) or check Instagram and TikTok handles. **Before Benn chooses any name, including keeping Tali, a trade mark attorney needs to run a clearance search.**

## 2. Method and what I could not check

- **Pool:** 64 names in four styles (real words, borrowed words, compounds, Tali variants). Section 6 lists them with the reason each one was cut.
- **Trade marks, US:** I queried the USPTO's own search back end for every name and counted live marks in classes 5, 9, 10, 35, 41, 42 and 44, on 11 Oct 2026. This is a word search for identical and containing marks. It does not cover phonetic or design similarity.
- **Trade marks, UK and EU:** the UK IPO search returned a Cloudflare challenge (HTTP 403). TMview returned an empty reply and EUIPO eSearch is a script-only page. So UK and EU findings come only from web search, which picks up UK Trade Mark Journal pages now and then. **Treat UK and EU as "not checked".**
- **Domains:** RDAP via rdap.org for .com, .co.uk, .app and .fit. "No record" (HTTP 404) *suggests* the name is unregistered. "Registered" (200) means someone holds it. I also loaded the registered sites to see what they serve. All of this is indicative only.
- **Apps:** web searches for App Store and Google Play listings, plus a direct look at the key listings. I did not search the stores' own apps by hand.
- **Social handles:** not checkable. TikTok returned 200 for a made-up handle and Instagram rate-limited every request. **Unknown for every name.**
- **Languages:** my own knowledge, not a native-speaker check. Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Polish, Hindi/Urdu, Arabic and Mandarin pinyin. The finalist needs a native-speaker check.
- **Not done:** Companies House (passing-off risk from UK businesses), app store search by hand, phonetic trade mark search.

### My read
- [I] **The UK case for keeping Tali is not hopeless, but it is unproven.** If an attorney finds no UK or EU TALI registration in classes 9, 42 or 44, and TALi is not actively trading in the UK, a UK-only launch as "Tali" carries moderate risk.

- [I] **Discoverability is a separate, real cost.** Someone searching "Tali" in the App Store will meet a clinician scribe app in the same category. That is fixable with a descriptor, but it never fully goes away.
- [I] **Timing matters more than anything.** Renaming before launch costs some web and app work and the www.tali.fit and app.tali.fit domains. Renaming after launch also costs users' trust, store reviews and any press. If there is doubt, decide now.

## R2.1 What "Tali" had (the sound profile used as a filter)

- **Two syllables, stress on the first** (TA-li).
- **Ends in a vowel**, which makes it open and friendly.
- **Soft consonants**: a liquid *l*, no hard clusters.
- **Sounds like a first name**: Tali is a Hebrew name meaning "dew".
- **Spellable after one hearing.**

I scored each candidate on these five points. A name had to have the meaning *and* most of the sound.

## R3.4 Honest view: is any of them as strong as Tali?

**No, not quite.** [I] Tali was unusually good:
- It hid the *exact product action* (keeping a tally) inside a soft name.
- The seam didn't show.
- It needed no explanation.

None of the round 3 names does all three as cleanly:
- **Tully** is the nearest in spirit. It is literally Tally's Irish twin, with a "quiet, peaceful" meaning underneath. But the echo is fainter, and **it sits close enough to TALI that it may not solve the problem that started this.** That is the first question for the attorney.
- **Pally** has the most natural echo and the warmest feel. But it is crowded in 2026 (an AI assistant, a "companion" class 9 filing) and can sound overfamiliar.
- **Daly** has the clearest echo. But that clarity makes it near-descriptive, and it owns no domain.

**What I'd do:**
1. **Get the attorney's UK and EU view on keeping Tali first.** If Tali is clearable in the UK, "Tali" with a store descriptor may still beat every alternative found in three rounds. That is a finding, not a failure.
2. If Tali is not clearable, put **Tully** (if the attorney says it is far enough from TALI) and **Pally** to the attorney, with **Tendi** as the clean fallback.
3. Run the waitlist "hear it once" test with **Tali as the control** against Tully, Pally and Daly. Ask:
   - "Spell it."
   - "What word do you hear in it?"
   - "Which feels most like a friend on your side?"
