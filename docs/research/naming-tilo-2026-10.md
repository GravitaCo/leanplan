# Tilo: deep dive (October 2026)

Author: `marketing-research` agent, for Benn. Checked 13 October 2026. Not committed. **Not legal advice.**
Labels: **[E]** the evidence shows, **[I]** my inference, **[G]** a guess that needs testing.
Context: Benn sees Tilo as the strongest name so far and wants Tali to work in the US eventually. US clearance therefore matters as much as UK and EU. Round 3 showed that the UK-ear check matters as much as the register check.

---

## Verdict

**Go to an attorney, with caution.** Tilo is clean in some places and not in others:

- **Clean:** there is no live US TILO mark in classes 9, 41 or 44. The one live US TILO (classes 37 and 42) covers architecture and construction.
- **Not clean:** the US has two near-identical marks for wellbeing apps, and the name has a real UK-ear weakness. I could not search the UK or EU registers at all, so the UK and EU picture is unknown, not clear.

**The three biggest risks**

1. **US: TILA and TEELO.**
   - **TILA** (US reg. 90667416, class 9, registered 5 Jul 2022) covers "mobile applications for tracking sleeping and providing meditation, and stress reduction guides for personal wellness". It belongs to the Mexican app *TILA: Sueño, Meditación y Calma*. *Tila* is Spanish for linden tea, which is the very story behind Tilo. The names are one letter apart and share the same meaning, for the same kind of app. [I] That is the strongest likelihood-of-confusion argument against Tilo in the US.
   - **TEELO** (US app. 99588085, classes 9 and 42, filed 11 Jan 2026 by a Paris-based individual) covers app software for "scheduling reminders, managing daily routines, monitoring movement and sleep data". It sounds identical to "TEE-lo", and **USPTO data shows it publishing for opposition on 27 Oct 2026.**
2. **UK ear: pronunciation and "kilo".**
   - People will say Tilo three ways: TEE-lo, TIE-lo and TIL-lo.
   - TEE-lo rhymes with **kilo**, and the spelling is one letter from it. That is a weight echo in a food app.
   - After one hearing, people will spell it in different ways (Teelo, Tylo, Tilo).
   - [I] Unlike Tali/tally, no everyday English word tied to the product comes through. The linden meaning has to be explained.
3. **A crowded small field and no good domain.**
   - "Tilo" is already used by:
     - a low-pressure, ADHD-friendly time-logging app (2025);
     - a "calm help for your iPhone" assistant in pilot;
     - an AI companion;
     - a Berlin stress app (GetTilo, which looks dormant);
     - an Austrian flooring company that owns tilo.com.
   - tilo.com, tilo.co.uk and tilo.app are all held; tilo.app is on a resale marketplace at an unseen price.
   - **tilo.fit looks free.**

**What would turn this into "proceed":**
- an attorney finds nothing in the UK or EU in classes 9, 41, 42 or 44;
- the attorney judges TILA and TEELO manageable in the US;
- the waitlist test shows most people settle on one pronunciation and don't hear "kilo".

## 6. UK-ear check (candid)

This is where Tilo is weakest. [I] Everything in this section is my judgement, and the waitlist test needs to confirm it.

- **Pronunciation splits three ways**, because English spelling gives no single answer:
  - **TEE-lo**, like *kilo*: the Spanish and German pronunciation, and probably what Benn intends.
  - **TIE-lo**, like *silo* or *Milo* (the malted drink): a natural reading for many UK speakers.
  - **TIL-lo**, like *pillow* or *willow*: also natural, and it brings out *till* and *Tilly*.
- **Which is most natural?** For UK readers I would expect a fairly even split between TIE-lo and TEE-lo, with TIL-lo a minority. [G] That needs testing.
- **Spelling after one hearing:**
  - TEE-lo gives Teelo, Tealo or Tilo.
  - TIE-lo gives Tylo or Tilo.
  - **Fails the Tali standard**: Tali was also ambiguous, but every spelling led back to the same word.
- **What a UK listener might half-hear:**
  - **"kilo"** (TEE-lo rhymes with it, and the spelling is one letter away): **the biggest concern for a food and body app.** Kilo Health is also a large maker of weight-loss and fasting apps [I, from knowledge, not checked].
  - **"tea" and "low"** (TEE-lo): calm, cosy, positive.
  - **"till"** (a shop till: counting money, which is close to the "tally" idea), **"tile"** (TIE-lo; ties to the flooring brand), **"Tilly"**, **"pillow"** and **"willow"** (TIL-lo; soft and calm), **"silo"** (TIE-lo; neutral to cold).
- **Brands nearby:** Milo (Nestlé drink; little known in the UK [I]), Tylö saunas, Tillo gift cards, Tile (Bluetooth tracker), Tilda rice.
- **Slang:** I know of no UK slang or regional meaning for "Tilo". [I]
- **Against Benn's round 3 insight:** Tilo does not carry an everyday English word tied to the product the way Tali carried "tally". Its meaning (linden, calming tea) has to be explained, like Hugi, Arki or Lagom. Benn may still prefer it, which is his call, but it is a different kind of name from Tali.

## 7. Fit

- **Friend on my side:** good. It is soft, two syllables, ends in a vowel and sounds like a first name. [I] Others are already using it for companions (an AI alien companion, a "calm help" assistant), which shows the sound reads as friendly.
- **Calm and steady:** strong *once explained*. Linden-flower tea (*tila*) is drunk as a "folk medicine and relaxant" in much of Europe (Wikipedia, *Tilia*). Spanish speakers get this unprompted.
- **Clear and trustworthy:** a good hidden story. [E] German villages held courts and assemblies under a **Gerichtslinde** ("court linden"), so a linden was a place where people met and matters were settled fairly (German Wikipedia, *Gerichtslinde*). The trust symbolism is my interpretation [I].
- **Mind, food, movement:**
  - Mind: calm (the tea; Bole "inner thought").
  - Food: a drink, and a tree that feeds bees.
  - Movement: weak. Lime avenues to walk under, and Chakavian "body", are a stretch.
- **Medical and weight-loss implications (for `compliance`):**
  - Linden tea is a traditional remedy for nerves and sleep. **Use the tree and tea as the name's origin story only.** Never say "Tilo calms you" or anything that implies a sedative or therapeutic effect (ASA/CAP; MHRA intended purpose).
  - The **"kilo" echo** is a soft weight cue. No copy should ever play on it.
  - Nothing in the name says diet, weight loss or tracking.
