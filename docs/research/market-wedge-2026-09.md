## 1. The answer in one minute

**Recommended wedge: "Keeping it."** UK adults who are stepping down from or coming off weight-loss medication, mostly bought privately, and want to keep their new eating and movement habits without going back to strict calorie counting. The job: *"when the extra help stops, help me keep what's working, calmly."* It fits Tali's frame closely. Appetite and "food noise" come back (mind), protein and flexible ranges matter (food), and strength training protects muscle (move). No one independent of a pharmacy serves this moment well in the UK.

**Confidence: medium-low.** The need and the gap are well evidenced. Whether people will pay, and whether we can reach them cheaply without breaking advertising rules, is not yet known. Section 7 lists the cheap tests.

**Single biggest risk:** we can't advertise to this group plainly. The ASA treats ads that mention "GLP-1" or "weight loss injections" as promoting prescription-only medicines. The group also includes people in psychological distress and some with eating-disorder risk. Growth has to come from communities, partners and word of mouth, and the safety design has to be excellent from day one.

Benn's three segments, in short: menopause is real pain but crowded and full of claim risk. Men in their 40s is a demographic, not a moment. "Tried and dropped tracking" is Tali's *positioning*, not a segment you can find.

## 3. The wedge in detail

### Who
UK adults, mostly aged 35 to 60, who used GLP-1 weight-loss medication (bought privately in most cases) and are tapering, have just stopped, or plan to stop within about three months. **[E]** GB use is about twice as common among women (4.0% vs 1.7%), peaks at ages 45 to 55 (4.2%), and is more common among people reporting psychological distress (UCL, *BMC Medicine*, Jan 2026). So this wedge reaches many perimenopausal women without Tali making any menopause claims **[I]**. The copy must stay gender-neutral.

### The moment and the job to be done
"The medicine was doing a lot of the work. Now my appetite is coming back, or I can't afford it, or I'm tapering. I don't want to go back to weighing every meal, and I'm scared of undoing it."

### How big, and why now
- **[E]** An estimated 1.6 million GB adults used weight-loss drugs between early 2024 and early 2025, and 3.3 million more said they were interested (UCL, Jan 2026).
- **[E]** The NHS priority cohort is 220,000 people over the first three years of tirzepatide's rollout (NICE / NHS England, 2025). So most users pay privately.
- **[E, US]** 64.8% of adults without type 2 diabetes stopped within a year (Rodriguez et al., *JAMA Netw Open*, 2025, n = 125,474).
- **[E]** Lilly raised the UK list price of Mounjaro by up to 170% from 1 September 2025. One provider's survey (CheqUp) claimed 80% would switch or stop. That is a company survey, so treat it as weak.
- **[G]** If UK stopping rates look anything like US ones, **several hundred thousand people come off each year**, perhaps 0.5 to 1 million. There are no UK stopping figures. This is the most important number to firm up.
- **[E]** Concentrated communities exist. r/mounjarouk has about 59k members and r/mounjaromaintenanceuk about 9k, growing about 25% a year. "Maintenance" is its most discussed topic (GummySearch, data dated 30 Sep 2026; I couldn't read Reddit directly).

### Why current options fail them
- **Their provider's app goes when the prescription goes.** [I] Voy, Juniper, Second Nature, WW and Noom tie coaching to the medication subscription. [E] Second Nature does offer a taper plan and a "no-regain guarantee", but only inside its programme (£229 to £299 a month by its own guide). [I] A provider earns more while you stay on the drug, so "life after" is never its best product.
- **The NHS aftercare doesn't reach private patients.** [E] NICE's 2025 quality standard says people should be monitored for at least a year after treatment, with structured follow-up. [I] That applies to NHS care, which most users aren't in.
- **General trackers are the burden they are trying to avoid.** [E] Diet apps lose about 86% of users within 100 days (Kidman et al., JMIR, Dec 2024). [E, weak] The top complaints in 1 to 3 star reviews of big trackers are paywalls, food database errors, ads and shame mechanics (unstar.app, method not disclosed). [E] On the GB App Store, MyFitnessPal's GLP-1 support is listed for the US and Canada only (release notes, 18 Aug).
- **Dose trackers stop being useful when the doses stop.** [E] Shotsy is rated 4.8 in GB but is built around injections.

### Why Tali can win (and what isn't a moat)
- **Mind first matches the real problem.** [I] What returns is appetite, cravings and stress eating, not a lack of information. Tali already asks about sleep, stress and room for change before it sets any targets.
- **Ranges, not limits, and honest ± margins.** [I] This is a calmer step down from strict counting. Gentle mode already hides numbers for anyone who needs that.
- **Strength and protein.** [E] ukactive and Les Mills (Dec 2025) urge strength training and enough protein for people on these drugs because of lean-mass loss. Tali's training engine builds home plans around time, equipment and body areas to go easy on.
- **Independent.** [I] Tali doesn't sell the drug, so it can honestly say "we're here whether you stay on it or not." A provider can't say that credibly.
- **UK-specific and offline.** [E] CoFID-first food data and UK chain menus, and it works with no signal.
- **Not a moat:** every one of these features can be copied. MyFitnessPal or Lose It! could ship a UK "maintenance" mode within a quarter. The durable part is **trust with this community, the tone, and being first in the UK conversation.** That's why the growth plan below leans on community and partners.

### Positioning line
> **Tali helps you keep what's working when the extra help stops. Sleep, food and movement in one calm place, with ranges, not rules.**

### Three example messages
These deliberately avoid drug names, "GLP-1", "injections" and any weight-loss promise. All need `compliance` sign-off, and ideally free CAP Copy Advice, before use.
1. "Appetite back? That's normal. Tali helps you plan for the hungry days instead of fighting them."
2. "Keep your strength. Short workouts at home and a simple protein range that fits your week."
3. "No streaks. No red numbers. Log a meal in seconds, or skip today. Tali will still be here."

## 6. Product gaps to win the wedge

1. **A "stepping down" start.** A short onboarding path for "I'm coming off or tapering". It starts at maintenance, sets a weekly rhythm (not daily), and ships ready-made if-then plans for hungry days. Storing medication status is special category data (today only the outcome is stored), so this goes to `compliance` and needs a DPIA update.
2. **Review the protein rule.** Today a GLP-1 medicine is grouped with kidney disease and insulin, which removes the high-protein anchor (`OnboardingOutcomes.medical` in `src/core/types.ts`). Muscle-preservation guidance points the other way for GLP-1 alone. Ask `nutrition-accuracy` whether to split the flag.
3. **Appetite and "food noise" in the check-in.** Add a light hunger and cravings signal to the mind check-in, with neutral copy (`mental-performance`).
4. **The weekly review and the 3 to 4 week weigh-in check.** The copy already promises it, but it isn't built. It's the heart of maintenance. Show a trend band, and hide it in gentle mode.
5. **Faster logging.** "Say or type the meal" (AI plan, phase A) and one-tap usual meals. Logging burden is the number one reason people quit.
6. **App store presence.** A PWA-only product can't be found in app stores, and iOS reminders need the app to be installed first. Plan a store build sooner.
7. **Fair pricing.** Monthly, cancel in two taps, no 12-month lock-in. [G] Something like £4 to £6 a month, since comparable trackers charge £35 to £65 a year. Remember that many people stopped the drug *because of cost*.
8. **Safety for this group.** Clinical review of the wellbeing question (already planned), plus extra care for people who used the drug with a healthy BMI.

## 7. Open questions and the cheapest way to answer each

| Question | Cheapest way to answer |
|---|---|
| Do people coming off want an app at all, or are they app-weary? | The 15 interviews (experiment 1) |
| Will they pay, and how much? | A short price question in the interviews and waitlist form; a clearly labelled "not live yet" price on the test page |
| How many people in the UK stop each year? | No public UK data found. Ask a partner pharmacy, or look for UK prescribing panel data (for example IQVIA) |
| Can we say who Tali is for in ads without it counting as promoting a medicine? | Ask the `compliance` agent, then use the ASA's free CAP Copy Advice service |
| Is the real moment "stopping" or "staying on a lower dose long term"? The Wegovy pill (MHRA approved 11 Jun 2026) and price changes could shift it | Interviews, plus a quarterly check of prices and news |
| Does "mind first" land, or does it sound like therapy? | Test message wording in the interviews |
| Do enough men come too, so the brand isn't boxed in? | Track the sex split in the pilot (users skew about 2:1 female) |
